Why generic marketing fails this trade

Where flooring companies lose growth

Every flooring job moves through four stages, and revenue can leak at any of them. Most flooring marketing reports stop at the lead. We follow it to the installed floor.

  1. 1 · The searchChosen on price

    Box stores, price shoppers and bid lists

    Homeowners compare per-square-foot prices and box-store installs online; commercial buyers compare bids. Win on price alone and margin shrinks. Your marketing has to show why your installation is worth more.

  2. 2 · The measureBooked isn't measured

    The most expensive hour you sell

    A measure, showroom visit or site walk that no-shows or reschedules costs a rep half a day you pay for anyway. Fast response, a confirmed appointment and a reminder protect it.

  3. 3 · The estimateMeasured isn't sold

    The estimate has to close

    The homeowner has your quote, a box-store quote and a sample on the floor; the GC has three bids. Clear options, follow-up and a firm install date decide it. Most agencies never see this step.

  4. 4 · The installSold isn't installed

    Revenue and margin land at the install

    Material lead times, subfloor and moisture surprises, and crew or subcontractor schedules can delay a job or cut its margin. We track sale to install, so marketing never outruns what your crews can deliver.

Install crews booked out three weeks? More flooring marketing isn't the fix.

When your crews are full, better use of that capacity beats more demand: aim marketing at higher-margin work, raise prices where demand supports it, tighten the schedule, or add a crew. And when capacity is the constraint, our answer to spending more on marketing is simple: Not yet.

Questions flooring companies ask

Flooring company marketing: the questions owners ask

How much should a flooring company spend on marketing?
There's no universal percentage. Agencies often quote 5–10% of revenue, but the number that matters is what it costs to win one sold job of the type you want, and whether the next dollar returns more than it costs. A company selling $4,700 hardwood installs can spend more to win each job than one selling $1,900 tile jobs and still come out ahead. Start with your job mix, close rate and margin, then set the budget.
What is the most effective marketing strategy for a flooring company?
A connected system, not one channel. Google Ads and Local Services Ads can bring inquiries quickly. SEO and your Google Business Profile take longer to build, then lower what every job costs to win. Reviews turn searchers into callers. Fast response and estimate follow-up decide how many of those inquiries become sold floors. Judge all of it by cost per sold job.
How do independent flooring companies compete with Home Depot and Lowe's?
Locally, you have advantages a big box can't copy. Google favors nearby businesses with strong reviews for "flooring installation [town]." You can show real projects from the homeowner's area, send an experienced person to measure, and give a firm install date and your own warranty. Market the installer, not just the material, and follow up faster than a retail counter.
Should a flooring company hire a marketing agency or handle marketing in-house?
Handle the basics yourself if you have time: your Google Business Profile, review requests and project photos. Hire help when ads, SEO and tracking need daily attention you can't give, or when you can't tell which channel produces sold jobs. Either way, own your accounts and data.
How do I know if my flooring company's marketing is actually working?
Track it past the lead. For each source you should know inquiries, measures booked, estimates sold, average sold job and gross profit. If your reports stop at clicks and leads, you can't tell a channel that sells hardwood from one that brings price shoppers. We report cost per sold job by channel and material every month.
