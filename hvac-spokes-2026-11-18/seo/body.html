What SEO means for an HVAC company

HVAC SEO is the work that gets your company found on Google without paying for each click: in the map results for “furnace repair near me” and in the organic results for searches like “heat pump installation [your city].” It’s one channel inside our HVAC marketing agency work, and like the rest, we judge it by the calls it produces and the jobs those calls sell.

SEO is slower than ads, but it keeps working when you stop paying. For an established HVAC company, it’s usually the channel that brings cost per sold job down over time.

How we do SEO for HVAC companies

  1. We set up your Google Business Profile the way Google expects. The right primary and secondary categories, every service you actually sell, a service area that matches where your trucks go, and an address shown only if customers come to you. If you work from a shop that homeowners never visit, Google’s rules say to hide it, and getting this wrong can cost you the listing.
  2. We keep reviews coming in every month. A review request goes out when the technician closes the job, and the reviews that mention the work and the town help the most. Recency matters as much as the total, so a company that collected most of its reviews three years ago tends to slip.
  3. We build a page for each service you want to sell. Replacements, heat pumps, ductless mini-splits, indoor air quality, maintenance plans and commercial work each get their own page that answers what a homeowner asks, from price ranges to rebates and financing. A single “Services” page can’t rank for all of them.
  4. We build a small number of real local pages, not hundreds of thin ones. Google’s spam policies call mass-produced, near-identical “[service] in [town]” pages scaled content abuse. We build pages for the towns that matter most to you, with real jobs, photos and local detail like utility rebates, and we’ll tell you if a past agency built pages that are now a risk.
  5. We earn links and mentions from your area. Local suppliers, manufacturer dealer listings, sponsorships, local news and community sites. A handful of real local links does more than a long list of directories.
  6. We measure SEO by sold jobs. Rankings and traffic are early signs. The report that matters shows the calls that came from Maps and organic search and what they sold, alongside what you spent.

The same approach is working outside HVAC: for a Tennessee plumbing company, it moved the average Google Maps position from 5.4 to 2.5. HVAC SEO: common questions

How long does SEO take for an HVAC company?

It depends on your starting point, your market and your competitors, so we don’t promise a timeline. Fixes to your Google Business Profile and reviews tend to show first in the map. New service pages and links take longer to build trust in the organic results.

How many reviews does an HVAC company need to rank in Google Maps?

There’s no fixed number. What matters is how you compare with the companies already in your local 3-pack, and how recent your reviews are. Look at the three companies Google shows for your main search: their review count and how many came in the last few months is the bar to clear.

Should we build a page for every town we serve?

No. Build pages for the towns that matter most to your business, and make each one genuinely local. Dozens of near-identical town pages can be treated as spam under Google’s policies and can drag down the rest of your site.

Is SEO or Google Ads better for an HVAC company?

Most established HVAC companies need both. Google Ads and LSAs bring calls now and can be turned up when crews have open days. SEO builds the calls you don’t pay for each time, which lowers your overall cost per sold job as it grows.
