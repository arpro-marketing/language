HVAC Google Ads and Local Services Ads: Bid on Installs, Not Clicks

By Andrew-Ryan Profaci · Updated November 18, 2026

Is your ad budget buying tune-ups while your install crews have open days?

Most HVAC ad accounts are judged by cost per lead. In that report a $99 tune-up call and a $7,500 system replacement count the same, so the account learns to buy whichever one is cheaper.

What does PPC look like for an HVAC company?

PPC for an HVAC company usually means two Google products. Local Services Ads sit at the very top of the page with the Google Guaranteed or Google Screened badge, and you pay for each lead. Search ads sit just below them, and you pay for each click. Both are part of our HVAC contractor marketing, and we measure both the same way: by the jobs they sell and the gross profit those jobs leave, not by the number of calls.

Paid search is the fastest channel an HVAC company has. A well-built campaign can ring the phone in its first week. It’s also the easiest to waste, because Google will happily spend a budget on whatever converts cheapest unless the account is told what a good call is actually worth.

Not every HVAC call is worth the same

An HVAC company sells three very different things through the same phone line, and they behave differently in an ad account.

Call typeTypical valueHow it behaves in ads
Repair and no-heat, no-coolAround $350 a visit (Angi)The most clicks and the cheapest leads. It spikes with heat waves and first freezes, and it is where a replacement conversation often starts.
System replacementAround $7,500 a system (Angi, 2026)Fewer searches and costlier clicks, and a longer path through a comfort visit and an estimate. One sale can pay for a month of ads.
Maintenance and tune-upsLow ticket, recurringCheap to buy and easy to book. It is worth paying for when it fills shoulder-month schedules or turns into a maintenance agreement, not when it crowds out install calls.

Here is why this matters. Say one account produces 100 leads a month at $50 each, mostly repairs, and sells two replacements. Another produces 60 leads at $80 each, aimed at replacement searches, and sells five. The first looks cheaper per lead. At $7,500 a system, the second brings in about $22,500 more in replacement revenue on almost the same spend. That’s an illustration of the math, not a client result.

Local Services Ads lean heavily on your review count, your rating and how fast you answer, so they reward the same work as your reviews and your call handling.

How we build an HVAC Google Ads account

  1. We separate the work by what it sells. Emergency repair, system replacement, heat pumps and ductless, and maintenance each get their own campaign and budget. That way a cold snap can’t quietly spend the replacement budget on no-heat calls, and you can see what each kind of call costs.
  2. We keep out the searches that never buy. Every account starts with a negative list built for HVAC: do-it-yourself and how-to searches, parts and filters, manuals and error codes, jobs and salaries, and free or warranty-only requests. It grows every week from the search terms report.
  3. We send each campaign to a page built for it. A replacement click lands on a page about replacements, with financing, the brands you install, real photos of your crews and one clear way to call or book. A no-cool click lands on a page that makes the next available visit easy to book.
  4. We send sold-job revenue back to Google. When your field software records the sale, that revenue goes back to the ad click that started it. ServiceTitan can do this with its Google Ads revenue import, and other systems can do it through offline conversion uploads. Google then learns which searches end in a $7,500 replacement instead of a $99 tune-up. Very few contractors have this in place: a scan of 1,000 contractor websites reported by Contracting Business in August 2026 found a Google Ads conversion tag on only 14% of them.
  5. We pace the spend to your season and your crews. Budgets follow demand and capacity. We push replacement campaigns when install crews have open days, pull back when they’re booked out, and use the shoulder months for maintenance agreements and replacement follow-ups.

You own the ad account, and we don’t mark up your ad spend. Our full approach to paid search for contractors is on our PPC for contractors page.

HVAC Google Ads and LSAs: common questions

How much do Google Ads cost for an HVAC company?

It depends on your market, your season and which calls you go after. Clicks for replacement searches cost more than repair clicks, and big metros cost more in July than smaller towns do in April. The number that matters is your cost per sold job by call type, because a cheap lead that never sells is the most expensive one you buy.

Are Local Services Ads worth it for HVAC companies?

For most established HVAC companies, yes, as long as someone answers every call fast and the reviews are strong. You pay per lead rather than per click, and the ads sit above everything else. Their weakness is control: you can’t choose keywords or landing pages, so they work best alongside search ads, not instead of them.

Should HVAC ads target repair calls or replacements?

Usually both, in separate campaigns with separate budgets. Repair calls keep technicians busy and often lead to a replacement conversation. Replacement campaigns cost more per lead but carry the margin. How much goes to each should follow your install capacity and what each campaign actually sells.

Can Google Ads use the revenue in ServiceTitan or Housecall Pro?

Yes. ServiceTitan has a built-in Google Ads revenue import that ties completed-job revenue to the original ad click, and other field software can send the same information through offline conversion uploads. Once that data is flowing, Google can bid toward sold jobs and their value instead of raw lead counts.
