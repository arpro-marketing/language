    The engine

    One proven spine, tuned for Persian

    The same eight mechanics drive every language on your ladder. What changes per language is the tuning — and Persian's tuning is the three decisions below. Everything here is calibrated for a speaking goal on a small, audio-heavy budget.

    1. Frequency-first vocabulary. The top ~1,000 words cover ~80% of everyday speech. Your SRS deck is built from a frequency list, not a textbook's chapter order.
    2. Comprehensible input at volume. Audio and video pitched just above your level (Krashen's i+1), scaled up hard as you climb. Persian has excellent learner-graded input.
    3. Active recall over passive review. You retrieve and produce — never re-read. Every SRS rep and every tutor sentence is retrieval under mild pressure.
    4. Output from week one. You speak before you feel "ready." A live tutor enters in Phase 1, not Phase 3. This is the step most self-learners skip and most need.
    5. Task-based sequencing. Organized around what you can do — greet, order, ask directions, tell a story — not around grammar tables.
    6. Sound + script front-loaded. The Perso-Arabic alphabet and the handful of new sounds get their own dedicated phase before vocabulary scales.
    7. Daily contact beats weekly blocks. A 30-minute Pimsleur lesson in the car every day outperforms one long Sunday session. Consistency is the whole game at this budget.
    8. CEFR checkpoints. A1 → A2 → B1 as hard, observable gates. You don't advance a phase on vibes; you advance on a demonstrated exit criterion.

    The three calls that make or break Persian

    Where a generic plan fails you

    Most "learn Persian" plans quietly optimize for reading and formal grammar. For a speaking goal that's the wrong target, and it costs learners months. These three decisions are the difference.

    Call 1 · Register

    Learn colloquial (spoken) Persian — not the written form

    Persian has a real split: written/formal (کتابی ketâbi) and colloquial/spoken (محاوره mohâvere, the Tehrani street standard). They differ in verb forms, pronouns, and even whole words. "I go" is می‌روم miravam in writing but می‌رم miram in speech. "Home" is خانه khâne written, خونه khune spoken.

    Textbooks teach formal first. If you follow them, you'll build sentences no one actually says out loud, then struggle to understand a normal conversation. Your primary sources (Pimsleur, Chai & Conversation) teach the spoken form by design — that's exactly why they anchor your stack.

    Verdict: spoken Persian is the target. Formal is a later add-on, not the foundation.
    Call 2 · Script

    Learn the script in parallel — never let it gate speaking

    The Perso-Arabic script is right-to-left and, like Arabic, normally omits short vowels — so early reading is slow. That's fine: reading is not your goal, speaking is. The trap is letting a hard script stall your conversation progress for months.

    So you run two tracks at once. Use a consistent transliteration ("Finglish") as a pronunciation crutch so your mouth keeps moving from day one — while you learn the alphabet on the side in Phase 0. By late Phase 1 the crutch falls away and the script unlocks native input.

    Verdict: script is a 2–3 week Phase 0 project, run beside speaking — not a prerequisite for it.
    Call 3 · Leverage the easy grammar

    Persian's grammar is your unfair advantage — lean on it

    Category III scares people, but that rating is weighted toward reading and script. The grammar itself is unusually kind to an English speaker: no grammatical gender, no noun cases, no adjective agreement, and strikingly regular verbs. Once you have the script and a few hundred words, you build real sentences fast.

    The two features to actually learn early are the ezâfe (an unstressed -e that links words: کتابِ من ketâb-e man, "book of mine") and the subjunctive, which Persian uses constantly ("I want to go" = mikhâm beram). Master those two and the language opens up.

    Verdict: front-load ezâfe + subjunctive; let the absence of gender/cases accelerate you.

    The path

    Four phases, each with an exit gate

    Week ranges assume ~5 focused hrs/wk plus daily audio. They flex with adherence — the audio-first multiplier is what compresses them. You advance only when you can demonstrate the gate. Your current phase is marked live.

    Pre-A1 · Foundations

    Sound & Script

    Weeks 1–3
    +

    Build the two things everything else sits on: the sound system and the alphabet. This is the one phase where you deliberately slow down before speeding up.

    Objectives

    • Produce every Persian sound, including the ones new to English: خ (kh), ق/غ (gh), the glottal ع/ء, and the tapped/rolled ر (r).
    • Recognize and write all 32 letters in their initial / medial / final / isolated forms.
    • Read short vowel-marked words slowly; lock a consistent transliteration system.
    • Hold your first scripted exchange: greet, give your name, say where you're from.

    What to run

    • Dead-time: Pimsleur Farsi L1, lessons 1–6 (one/day, in the car or gym).
    • Focused: alphabet via a dedicated script resource — Easy Persian's alphabet series (free) or a "Read & Write Persian" workbook. 20 min/day.
    • Chai & Conversation: lessons 1–3, following the free lesson guides.
    • Start a tiny Anki deck: greetings, pronouns, the ~30 starter words below.
    Exit gate → Phase 1

    You can sound out a written word unaided (slowly), reproduce all four "hard" sounds, recognize the full alphabet, and run a 4-line self-introduction from memory. ~50 words active.

    ۱
    A1 · Survival speaking

    Predictable Everyday

    Weeks 4–20
    +

    Get functional in the predictable situations: greetings and small talk, introducing yourself, ordering, shopping, simple directions, numbers and prices. Present tense does most of the work. This is where you start speaking with a human — a tutor enters around week 6, not "someday."

    Language targets

    • ~300–500 active words (frequency-ordered).
    • Present tense of the high-use verbs: budan (be), dâshtan (have), khâstan (want), raftan (go), khordan (eat), kardan (do).
    • The ezâfe link; plurals with ‑hâ; negation (ne-/na-); possessive suffixes (-am, -et, -esh).
    • Question words: chi, kojâ, key, ki, cherâ, chetor, chand.

    What to run

    • Dead-time: finish Pimsleur L1 (7–30), begin L2.
    • Input: Chai & Conversation, working sequentially with lesson guides.
    • SRS: Anki, 10 new cards/day + reviews (~15 min).
    • Output: iTalki/Preply tutor — start weekly ~week 6. Bring the week's phrases and force yourself to use them live.
    Exit gate → Phase 2

    You can introduce yourself, ask and answer basic questions, order/shop, and survive a slow scripted exchange with a patient speaker. ~500 words active. At least 4 live tutor sessions completed.

    ۲
    A2 · Functional conversation

    Off the Script

    Weeks 20–52
    +

    Move from scripted lines to flexible everyday conversation: talk about your day, your past, your plans; express preferences and opinions; handle the unexpected turn. You also meet târof — the Persian ritual of polite offering and refusal — which you cannot skip and stay socially fluent.

    Language targets

    • ~1,000–1,500 active words.
    • Past tense and future; the subjunctive in earnest (mikhâm beram, bâyad beram).
    • Compound verbs — Persian's core verb machinery (kâr kardan = to work, dust dâshtan = to like).
    • Connectors and discourse glue: چون chun (because), واسه‌ی اینکه vâse-ye inke (because / so that), ولی vali (but), بعدش ba'desh (then / after that).
    • Working grasp of târof phrases.

    What to run

    • Dead-time: finish Pimsleur L2; re-listen to older lessons at speed.
    • Input: Chai & Conversation intermediate; Easy Persian video; slow-Persian YouTube with subtitles.
    • SRS: shift toward sentence cards mined from your lessons, not just single words.
    • Output: weekly tutor + a HelloTalk/Tandem exchange partner for free extra reps.
    Exit gate → Phase 3

    You can navigate most everyday interactions, narrate past events and future plans, and hold a ~15-minute tutor chat that mostly stays in Persian. ~1,500 words active.

    ۳
    B1 · Conversational — the goal

    Real, Unscripted Talk

    Year 2
    +

    The target state: sustain a genuine, unscripted conversation on familiar topics, follow a native speaking at normal colloquial speed, and express and defend an opinion. Hitting this gate is the trigger to drop Persian to maintenance and open French.

    Language targets

    • ~2,500+ active words; comfort with idiom and colloquial contractions.
    • Understand fast native speech on familiar topics without a transcript.
    • Speak in connected paragraphs, not just sentences — tell a story, give reasons.

    What to run

    • Input-heavy: Persian podcasts, YouTube, and shows/films with subtitles; use Language Reactor to mine sentences.
    • Output: 2× weekly tutor; keep a short spoken/written journal in Persian.
    • SRS: cards built entirely from your own mined sentences.
    Exit gate → maintenance + unlock French

    You sustain a 30-minute conversation, understand a native talking normally on familiar topics, and can state and back up an opinion. Persian drops to ~30 min/week upkeep; French unseals.

    The weekly operating system

    How the <5 hours actually get spent

    The move that defeats your time budget: audio-first reclaims dead time. Pimsleur runs while you drive, train, or do chores — hours that cost you nothing. That's why only ~5 hours are "sit-down," but your effective volume runs 8–10 hours a week. Protect the daily audio lesson above everything else; if a day collapses, the audio is the thing that still happens.

    BlockWhen / howWeekly load
    Pimsleur lesson Dead-timeOne 30-min lesson daily — car, gym, walking, chores~3.5 hrs (free time)
    Anki SRS Focused15 min/day, phone, first thing or in a queue~1.5 hrs
    Chai & Conversation Focused2 lessons/week with the lesson guide + notes~1 hr
    Live tutor (iTalki) Focused1 session/week, 30–45 min — the non-negotiable~0.75 hr
    Script / input FocusedAlphabet drills early; comprehensible input later~0.75 hr

    Focused total ≈ 4 hrs of sit-down work + ~3.5 hrs of reclaimed audio = ~7.5–10 effective hrs. If you can only protect one focused block, keep the tutor — speaking is the goal and it's the rep you can't fake.

    The orchestrated stack

    Six tools, each with one job

    No single app is the moat — the sequencing is. Each tool below does one thing well; the leverage is running them together in the right order. Verified current — Aug 2026.

    Speaking spine · dead-time

    Pimsleur Farsi (L1 + L2)

    60 audio lessons of Iranian Persian (two levels, 30 each), 30 min per lesson, spoken-first with no drills. Your daily reclaimed-time engine. Level 1 also introduces the alphabet at the end.

    Colloquial backbone · input + lessons

    Chai & Conversation

    Structured lessons in spoken conversational Persian with free lesson guides, plus culture and târof. Podcast is free; the paid platform adds workbooks and review.

    Podcast free
    Vocabulary engine · SRS

    Anki + a Persian frequency deck

    Spaced repetition on a frequency-ordered deck (search AnkiWeb for a Persian/Farsi top-1000 deck, or build from a frequency list). Later, cards from your own mined sentences.

    Free (desktop)
    Output layer · the one people skip

    iTalki / Preply

    Live Persian tutors, cheap, on demand. This is the rep that turns passive knowledge into speech. Weekly from Phase 1, twice-weekly by Phase 3.

    Free reps · exchange

    HelloTalk / Tandem

    Text and voice exchange with native speakers. Free volume between tutor sessions; good for low-stakes daily output.
