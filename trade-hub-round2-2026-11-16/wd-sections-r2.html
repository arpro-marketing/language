Before you book

The questions window and door owners actually ask about marketing

What is the best marketing for window and door contractors?
Capturing homeowners who are already searching, in the order it usually pays: sub-five-minute lead response and the in-home estimate path (pays this week on the leads you already have), Google Ads and Local Services Ads for install-intent searches (pays in weeks), a lead engine that produces homeowners who contacted you alone (pays as the channel mix shifts), a website engineered to turn researchers into estimate requests (lifts every channel at once), a review engine that compounds referrals, and a fully built Google Business Profile and local SEO to own the map pack (slowest to pay, most durable once it does). Social branding and demand creation come after the engine is paying for them: capturing the homeowner already searching beats manufacturing attention.
How much should a window and door company spend on marketing?
There is no universal percentage. It depends on your revenue stage, market saturation and growth goal, not a flat "5–10% of revenue" rule. A company under $1M invests differently than one scaling past $3M. The better question is what it costs to book one job today, and whether more spend on the channels already producing sold jobs would return more than it costs.
Should a window and door company run Google Ads or SEO?
Both, in sequence: they solve different problems. Paid search turns on estimate requests within weeks but costs money every month; SEO takes a few months to build but compounds and lowers your blended cost per job over time. Most window and door companies use paid search to fund growth while SEO matures underneath it. Anyone promising first-page SEO in 30 days is describing paid ads.
How do window and door companies get more installation jobs, not just more leads?
By fixing the part of the funnel that loses the most: usually what happens after the form. Response inside five minutes, a confirmed appointment, financing made clear before the visit, and a no-show prevented add jobs without adding a single lead. Then by generating your own leads instead of buying shared ones, and by measuring to the sold job so budget moves toward the channel actually producing installs.
What is the second funnel in window and door marketing?
The first funnel gets the homeowner to reach out: SEO, Google Maps, ads and your website. The second funnel is everything after: how fast the inquiry is answered, whether the in-home estimate gets booked and kept, and whether the estimate is followed up and signed. Most window and door marketing only works on the first. We measure and improve both, because a lost estimate costs as much as an inquiry you never got.
What makes marketing for window and door companies different from other contractors?
The economics. Window and door is a high-ticket, long-cycle purchase, a five-figure job a homeowner makes once every 15 to 40 years, so the in-home estimate is where deals are won or lost, and every lead is worth far more than in a high-frequency trade. Marketing that ignores the estimate-to-sale path leaves most of the money on the table.
How do I choose a window and door marketing agency?
Three things matter: leads that come to you exclusively rather than through a shared queue, attribution that ties spend to booked jobs rather than clicks, and real specialization: not a logo wall, but whether the agency can walk your funnel in your trade's numbers (ticket size, buyer cycle, the in-home estimate) without reaching for a template. Then ask two questions: "Show me named results with real numbers," and "What happens when something isn't working?" Ask us both: you get a named dealership operating record with the owner's number, monthly reporting that starts at cost per booked job, and terms with a way out: smaller engagements run month to month after the first 3 to 6 months, and 12-month engagements carry a 90-day exit.
