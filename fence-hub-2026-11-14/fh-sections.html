The questions fence company owners actually ask about marketing

What is the best marketing for fence companies?
Capturing homeowners who are already searching, in the order it usually pays: fast response and quote follow-up on the inquiries you already get (pays this week), Google Ads and Local Services Ads for install-intent searches like "privacy fence installation" (pays in weeks), a website that turns researchers into quote requests (lifts every channel), a review engine that turns every finished fence into the next job, and a fully built Google Business Profile and local SEO to own the map pack in your zip codes (slowest to pay, most durable once it does). Organic social video builds your brand cheaply, but it works best after the search engine is paying for itself.
How much does marketing for fencing companies cost?
Most fencing companies invest $2,000–$5,500 a month for a complete marketing system (SEO, lead generation and conversion work), plus ad spend. As a share of revenue, contractors holding steady demand typically spend 3–5% of gross revenue, and contractors in aggressive growth mode 8–12%. A percentage is only a starting point: the better question is what it costs to sell one fence today, and whether the next dollar returns more than it costs. A single $4,000–$12,000 install can cover one to several months of marketing, and cost per lead drops as organic rankings strengthen. Budget allocation for fence companies

How much does a fence contractor lead cost?
By channel: Google Local Services Ads run about $25–$65 per fencing lead in most markets (about $71 averaged across home services, The Media Captain, 2025), Google Ads $40–$60 per lead ($15–$50 per click), Facebook ads around $23 per lead, and shared marketplace leads $15–$25. Organic SEO costs $0 per lead once rankings are established; the investment is the monthly work. The number that matters more is cost per closed job: a $40 organic lead at a 35% close rate costs about $114 per closed job, while a $30 shared lead at 8% costs about $375. We track every source to closed revenue so your marketing budget follows actual return, not gut feel.
Is Angi worth it for fence contractors?
For most fence contractors, Angi and similar marketplaces produce diminishing returns because the leads are shared: sold to several contractors at once, with close rates around 5–15%. Exclusive inquiries from your own website, Google Business Profile and ads typically close at 25–40%. The cost per lead may look similar on paper; the cost per closed job is dramatically different. We build systems that generate leads you own instead of renting them. Building an owned lead generation system
Should a fencing company use Google Ads or SEO, and when should it start?
Both, in sequence. Google Ads delivers visibility now, which matters heading into the spring and summer peak; SEO builds organic visibility that keeps producing calls without paying for every click. Most companies start with both and scale paid back as rankings strengthen. Timing matters as much as the channel: start in late fall or winter, because SEO takes 60 to 90 days to gain traction, fence searches jump roughly 20% in March and peak by June, and companies that start in April are months behind. How long does fencing SEO take, and who does it bring in?
Most fencing companies see measurable ranking improvements within 60 to 90 days and consistent calls within 4 to 6 months; Google Business Profile work often moves the map pack within weeks. The timeline depends on local competition, how established your site is and how wide your service area is. The inquiries come from homeowners and property managers actively searching for fence installation, repair or replacement in your area, people who have already decided they need a fence, which is why exclusive organic leads typically close at 25 to 40 percent. What types of fencing projects get searched most, and which pay most?
Privacy fence installation, wood fence replacement, vinyl and aluminum fencing, and chain link for commercial properties generate the most search volume. Wood accounts for about 65% of residential demand and vinyl about 25% (ArcSite, 2025). Value runs the other way: chain link $1,500–$3,000, wood $3,000–$6,000, vinyl $4,000–$8,000, privacy and premium $6,000–$12,000 (HomeGuide, 2026). Pool, pet containment and estate fencing are lower volume but higher value per job. Homeowners buy mainly for privacy (45%), security (30%) and pets (20%) (ArcSite, 2025), so marketing should speak to those reasons, not just list materials. What is the second funnel in fence company marketing?
The first funnel gets the homeowner to reach out: SEO, Google Maps, ads and your website. The second funnel is everything after: how fast the inquiry is answered, whether the measure appointment gets booked and kept, how fast the quote goes out, and whether it's followed up and signed. Most fence marketing only works on the first. We measure and improve both, because a lost quote costs as much as an inquiry you never got.
How do I get commercial fencing contracts?
Commercial work, such as industrial perimeter security, HOA community fencing, multi-unit residential and municipal installs, usually carries higher contract values, and it's marketed differently. Decision-makers search terms like "commercial fence contractor" and "perimeter security fencing" and judge you on licensing, insurance limits, a portfolio of commercial work and capacity for large projects. We build service pages for commercial intent, set up your Google Business Profile for commercial categories, and create project content that shows your commercial capability. Residential versus commercial positioning
Do I need a website, or is my Google Business Profile enough?
Your Google Business Profile is critical for the map pack, but it's rented space: Google controls what appears and can change the rules any time. When a homeowner views a well-built profile, about 48% click through to the website, 21% call from the listing and 9% ask for directions. The ones who click are checking your gallery, license and insurance before a $6,000–$10,000 decision, so a weak or missing site loses nearly half your potential customers. The site needs to work on a phone first (84% of "near me" searches are mobile, and 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes over 3 seconds): galleries by fence type with real photos, license, insurance, years in business and service area above the fold, tap-to-call on every page, a four- or five-field quote form, and a page for each town you serve. How your website and profile work together
How do I show up on Google Maps, and how much do reviews matter?
The map pack is where most local fence calls start: 42% of local searchers click within it. Three factors drive rank. Relevance: "Fence Contractor" as the primary category and every service listed (privacy, wood, vinyl, chain link, commercial). Distance: an accurate verified address and service area. Prominence: review count and rating, consistent name, address and phone across the web, fencing content on your site and local citations. Reviews do double duty: recent, highly rated reviews lift map rank, and reviews that mention "privacy fence" or "vinyl fence installation" tie you to those searches. Side by side, the company with 47 five-star reviews beats the one with 8 nearly every time, and 95% of consumers read reviews before they buy. We build review collection that runs without you having to remember to ask. Map pack and review strategy
Should I hire a marketing agency or do my own marketing?
The deciding factor is opportunity cost. If your time is worth $75–$150 an hour installing or selling fences, every hour spent learning Google Ads is an hour not spent closing an $8,000 contract. Do-it-yourself marketing works if you have more time than money; an agency works if you have more money than time, and only if it reports revenue (cost per lead, cost per closed job, return) instead of impressions and rankings. How do I compete with bigger fence companies that have more reviews and a bigger budget?
You don't need a bigger budget, you need a sharper one. Large companies spread across whole metros and many service lines, so they can't out-focus you in your territory. Dominate a tighter geography: own the top map positions in your core zip codes. Own a niche: ornamental iron, automated gates or commercial perimeter work, where specialists beat generalists. And out-respond them: owners who answer fast and show up to quotes personally close at higher rates than call centers with ten times the ad budget. Out-positioning larger competitors

Three things matter: inquiries that come to you alone rather than through a shared queue, attribution that ties spend to sold jobs rather than clicks, and real fence knowledge: whether the agency can walk your funnel in your trade's numbers (job value by fence type, the three-quote buyer, the spring rush and crew capacity) without reaching for a template. Then ask two questions: "Show me named results with real numbers," and "What happens when something isn't working?"
