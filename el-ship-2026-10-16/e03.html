What does effective electrician marketing look like in 2026?

Effective electrician marketing starts with the job mix, not the lead count. The average electrical job in the US is $351, but a panel upgrade runs about $1,600 and a standby generator averages $5,157 (Angi 2026). The same marketing dollar can buy either one. A strong system does three things. It gets the company found for the work it wants more of, gets every call answered and booked, and turns estimates into signed installs the crews have room for. Measure it by what’s installed and paid for, by job type, and the next dollar goes where it earns the most, not where the last agency pitched.

The numbers behind it: Google Local Services Ads for electricians cost about $39 a lead, and 43.4% of them book a job (SearchLight, February 2026, 112 electrical accounts). That’s about $90 per booked call. A booked call isn’t a sold job, though. A service visit is paid the day it’s done. A panel or generator still has to be estimated, sold, permitted and installed. Most electrician marketing is never measured past the call.
