Electrical contractor marketing by job type: how each one is bought

Six kinds of electrical work, six different buyers. Marketing that works for one can waste money on the next.

Service and emergency repairs

Bought in hours

The homeowner calls whoever answers. Win it with the map pack, Local Services Ads and a phone that’s picked up on the first ring. The real value is the panel or rewire the tech spots on the visit.

Panel and service upgrades

Bought in days to weeks

Often triggered by an inspection, a home sale, an addition or a new appliance. Win it with search pages for the upgrade itself, a fast estimate and clear pricing ranges. About $1,600 on average (Angi 2026).

Standby generators

Bought over weeks, and in a rush after an outage

Researched, compared and financed, then demand spikes after every storm. The average customer lost about 11 hours of power in 2024, the most in ten years (EIA), and Helene hit East Tennessee. Win it with content that answers sizing and cost questions, a follow-up sequence and capacity planned before the season. $5,157 average (Angi 2026).

Rewiring and renovation work

Bought through the job it’s part of

Older homes, remodels and additions. The median owner-occupied home is 42 years old (NAHB, 2024). Win it with remodeler and realtor referrals, plus pages for older-home wiring. A full rewire runs about $5,000–$10,000 (Angi, $2–$4 a square foot).

EV chargers

Bought with the car

A Level 2 install averages $967, plus $800–$2,000 when it needs a panel upgrade (Angi 2026). The federal charger credit ended June 30, 2026, so sell the install and the panel check, not a rebate.

Lighting and smart-home upgrades

Bought when the budget allows

Lower urgency, higher-budget homeowners. Best as an add-on to work you’re already doing in the house, not a paid-ads priority.
