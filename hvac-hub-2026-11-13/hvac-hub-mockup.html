Why generic marketing fails this trade

Where HVAC companies lose growth

Every HVAC job moves through four stages, and revenue can leak at any of them. Most HVAC marketing reports stop at the call. We follow it to the installed system.

  1. 1 · The searchChosen on speed and trust

    Emergencies, price shoppers and franchises

    A homeowner with no heat calls whoever shows up first with good reviews. One planning a replacement gets three quotes, often including a franchise. Your marketing has to win both.

  2. 2 · The callCalled isn't booked

    The busiest week is when calls get missed

    A heat wave or the first cold snap floods the phones, and every missed or slow call goes to a competitor. How many calls get booked, and how fast, decides what your marketing buys.

  3. 3 · The estimateQuoted isn't sold

    The estimate has to close

    The homeowner has your quote, two others and a financing question. Clear options, a confident comfort advisor and steady follow-up decide it. Most agencies never see this step.

  4. 4 · The installSold isn't installed

    Revenue and margin land at the install

    Equipment lead times, permits, ductwork surprises and peak-season crew schedules can delay a job or cut its margin. We track sale to install, so marketing never outruns what your crews can deliver.

Install crews booked out two weeks in July? More HVAC marketing isn't the fix.

When your crews are full, better use of that capacity beats more demand: aim marketing at higher-margin work, raise prices where demand supports it, tighten the schedule, or add a crew. In the quiet shoulder months, your own customer base usually beats new ad spend. And when capacity is the constraint, our answer to spending more on marketing is simple: Not yet.

Questions HVAC companies ask

HVAC marketing: the questions owners ask

How much does marketing cost for an HVAC company?
There's no universal percentage. Agencies often quote 5–10% of revenue, but what matters is what it costs to win one sold job of the type you want. A company selling $7,500 system replacements can spend more per job than one living on $350 repair calls and still come out ahead. Start with your job mix, close rate and margin, then set the budget.
What is the best marketing strategy for HVAC companies?
A connected system, not one channel. Google Ads and Local Services Ads bring calls quickly. SEO and your Google Business Profile take longer, then lower what every job costs to win. Reviews turn searchers into callers, and maintenance plans bring repeat work. Fast call handling and estimate follow-up decide how many calls become sold jobs. Judge it all by cost per sold job and gross profit.
What is the second funnel in HVAC marketing?
The first funnel gets the phone to ring: SEO, Google Maps, ads and your website. The second funnel is everything after: how fast the call is answered, whether it books, whether the comfort visit happens, and whether the estimate is followed up and sold. Most HVAC marketing only works on the first. We measure and improve both, because a lost estimate costs as much as a call you never got.
How do I get HVAC leads during the off-season?
Start with the customers you already have: maintenance-plan tune-ups, systems your techs flagged as aging, and open estimates from the busy season. Then market jobs that don't depend on the weather, like indoor air quality, ductless and planned replacements with financing. Keep reviews coming all year so you're on top when the weather turns.
How important are Google reviews for HVAC companies?
Very. Searches like "AC repair near me" show the Google map results near the top, and homeowners compare ratings and recent reviews before they call. Steady new reviews, with your replies, help you rank in Maps and win the call. Ask automatically after every service call and install.
Should an HVAC company focus on repair leads or installation leads?
Both. Repair calls keep techs busy, build trust and start many replacements. Replacements and new installs carry most of the revenue and gross profit. The right mix depends on your close rate, margin and install capacity, so we track each job type separately.
How long does HVAC SEO take to produce results?
Google Business Profile and review work often moves map rankings within weeks. Service and town pages usually take three to six months, longer in big metros. Ads bring calls while SEO builds, and fixing call handling and follow-up pays from the first month.
