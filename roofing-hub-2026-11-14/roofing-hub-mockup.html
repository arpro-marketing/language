The questions roofing owners actually ask about marketing

What is the best marketing strategy for roofing companies?
Capturing homeowners who are already searching, in the order it usually pays: fast call response and a booked inspection (pays this week on the leads you already have), Google Ads and Local Services Ads for replacement and repair searches (pays in weeks), a website that turns searches into inspection requests (lifts every channel at once), a review engine that compounds referrals, and a fully built Google Business Profile and local SEO to own the map pack (slowest to pay, most durable once it does). Most roofing companies over-invest in one channel and ignore the others. The system matters more than any single tactic.
How much should a roofing company spend on marketing?
There is no universal percentage. It depends on your revenue stage, market saturation and growth goal, not a flat "5–10% of revenue" rule. A roofing company under $1M invests differently than one scaling past $5M. The better question is what it costs to sign one roof today, and whether more spend on the channels already producing signed jobs would return more than it costs. With an average roof replacement near $9,611 (Angi, 2026), one extra signed job a month changes the math quickly.
Should a roofing company run Google Ads or SEO?
Both, in sequence: they solve different problems. Google Ads and Local Services Ads turn on inspection requests within weeks but cost money every month, and they catch the spike in searches after a storm. Roofing is one of the more competitive local SEO markets, so SEO usually takes 3 to 6 months to produce steady calls, while Google Business Profile improvements often show up within 30 to 60 days. Once you rank, organic calls cost almost nothing extra and close at higher rates. Anyone promising first-page SEO in 30 days is describing paid ads.
How do I get roofing leads without buying them from lead services?
Build a lead engine you own. When a homeowner searches for a roofer and finds your website or map listing, that call goes only to you, not to four other roofers. Local SEO, a complete Google Business Profile and a website built to turn visitors into inspection requests create exclusive leads every month without per-lead fees. Those leads compound over time instead of stopping the day you stop paying.
How important are Google reviews for roofing companies?
Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking factors and one of the biggest reasons a homeowner calls one roofer over another. Homeowners spending five figures on a roof check reviews before they call. A steady review process, every customer asked after every roof and every review answered, is a core part of roofing marketing, not an extra.
Does storm chasing work as a long-term roofing marketing strategy?
Storm chasing brings short bursts of volume but doesn't build a lasting business. Homeowners in storm-hit areas increasingly research roofers online before signing, and insurers look at claims more closely. A roofing company that already ranks locally and has strong reviews catches storm demand without the cost of canvassing crews and travel. The best position is the trusted local roofer who also benefits from storm season, not a company that depends on it.
What is the second funnel in roofing marketing?
The first funnel gets the homeowner to call: SEO, Google Maps, ads and your website. The second funnel is everything after: how fast the call is answered, whether the inspection gets booked and kept, and whether the estimate is followed up and signed, including the homeowner waiting on financing or an adjuster. Most roofing marketing only works on the first. We measure and improve both, because a lost estimate costs as much as a call you never got.
How do I choose a roofing marketing agency?
Three things matter: leads that come to you exclusively rather than through a shared queue, reporting that ties spend to signed jobs rather than clicks, and real understanding of your trade: whether the agency can walk your funnel in roofing numbers (ticket size, storm swings, the inspection, crew capacity) without reaching for a template. Then ask two questions: "Show me named results with real numbers," and "What happens when something isn't working?" Ask us both: you get a named dealership operating record with the owner's number, monthly reporting that starts at cost per signed job, and terms with a way out: smaller engagements run month to month after the first 3 to 6 months, and 12-month engagements carry a 90-day exit.
